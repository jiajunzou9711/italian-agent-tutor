# Adaptive Italian Syllabus / 自适应意大利语路线

## Goal

Build usable written Italian around the learner's stated purpose. The route is mastery-driven rather than calendar-driven.

## Direction

`A1 → A2 → B1 → B2`

- **A1:** identity, possession, routines, location, food, shopping, time, travel, invitations, simple problems, first past reference.
- **A2:** connected daily communication, past experience, plans, services, pronouns, comparison, habitual background, short messages.
- **B1:** narration, reasons and opinions, practical independence, accessible articles, connected 100–150-word writing.
- **B2:** detailed explanation, arguments, register, complex tense relationships, authentic written material.

## Unit progression

Start at the first unit for a beginner or at the earliest unfinished unit in the placement-supported route. Work through that route in order, without a calendar. Within a unit, choose scenes and vocabulary that serve the learner's goal. Preserve stronger skills demonstrated at placement and handle isolated earlier gaps in review.

For each A1 unit, turn its grammar and functional skills into a short list of testable ledger points tagged U1–U12. Define the required points before counting progress and keep that scope stable as practice proceeds. Vocabulary examples are a pool for practice. For A2–B2, use the stage direction and learner goal to define ordered, named units with a short list of points and an observable written Output task before starting each one.

A unit is complete when every required point is at `Pending` or above and its Output task has been completed independently, with meaning, key details, and task completion intact. Earlier repair points are outside that unit's exit criteria.

## A1 units

### Unit 1: Identity and Basic Description

- Practical goal: introduce yourself and identify other people.
- Grammar: subject pronouns when needed; `essere`; basic negation; singular and plural agreement.
- Vocabulary: name, nationality, language, student, teacher, city, country, basic adjectives.
- Output: produce short self-introductions and simple identity contrasts.

### Unit 2: Possession, Family, and Personal Objects

- Practical goal: say what you have and describe a small personal world.
- Grammar: `avere`; `essere` versus `avere`; indefinite articles; basic noun gender and plural forms.
- Vocabulary: family members, phone, bag, ticket, keys, book, home objects, numbers.
- Output: describe possessions, family members, and simple missing items.

### Unit 3: Everyday Actions and Habits

- Practical goal: describe regular activities and answer simple routine questions.
- Grammar: present tense of common regular verbs; frequency words; basic negation with action verbs.
- Vocabulary: study, work, eat, drink, play, watch, read, write, morning, evening, weekdays.
- Output: describe a normal day in several short sentences.

### Unit 4: Home, Neighborhood, and Location

- Practical goal: say where people and objects are and describe familiar places.
- Grammar: common location phrases; basic uses of `a`, `in`, and articulated forms such as `al` and `alla`; `c'è` and `ci sono` as a first introduction.
- Vocabulary: home, room, kitchen, bar, school, station, park, street, supermarket, nearby places.
- Output: describe where you are, where an object is, and what exists in a place.

### Unit 5: Food, Drinks, and Ordering

- Practical goal: order simple food and drinks and express basic preferences.
- Grammar: definite articles; common quantity expressions; first use of `volere` and polite request patterns.
- Vocabulary: meals, common dishes, drinks, menu words, prices, basic taste adjectives.
- Output: complete a short café or restaurant exchange.

### Unit 6: Shopping and Numbers

- Practical goal: ask about price, quantity, size, and simple product choices.
- Grammar: adjective agreement; demonstratives; numbers; question words such as `quanto`, `quale`, and `dove`.
- Vocabulary: clothing, colors, sizes, money, shop items, numbers, useful service expressions.
- Output: ask for an item, compare simple options, and understand a basic purchase exchange.

### Unit 7: Time, Calendar, and Appointments

- Practical goal: discuss time, dates, schedules, and simple plans.
- Grammar: telling time; days and months; common time prepositions; present tense for scheduled events.
- Vocabulary: clock time, days, months, today, tomorrow, weekend, appointment, opening and closing times.
- Output: arrange a meeting and describe a simple weekly schedule.

### Unit 8: City Travel and Directions

- Practical goal: move around a city and ask for practical travel information.
- Grammar: `andare`, `venire`, and common movement expressions; first imperative patterns for simple directions; location review.
- Vocabulary: transport, station, stop, ticket, left, right, straight ahead, near, far, city landmarks.
- Output: ask for and understand basic directions and travel information.

### Unit 9: Friends, Interests, and Invitations

- Practical goal: talk about hobbies and make simple social plans.
- Grammar: `piacere` as an introduction; `potere`, `volere`, and common infinitive patterns; question-answer exchanges.
- Vocabulary: sports, films, music, free time, invitations, meeting places, weekend activities.
- Output: invite someone, accept or decline, and discuss interests.

### Unit 10: Health and Everyday Problems

- Practical goal: explain a simple problem and ask for basic help.
- Grammar: `stare`; common expressions with `avere`; polite requests; useful negative forms.
- Vocabulary: body parts, common symptoms, pharmacy, doctor, lost items, delays, simple emergencies.
- Output: explain a minor health issue or everyday problem in clear short sentences.

### Unit 11: Recent Experiences

- Practical goal: begin talking about completed personal events.
- Grammar: first introduction to `passato prossimo` with a controlled set of high-frequency verbs.
- Vocabulary: yesterday, last week, travel, meals, study, work, leisure, common past-event markers.
- Output: write a short account of what happened yesterday.

### Unit 12: A1 Consolidation

- Practical goal: combine familiar language across several everyday situations.
- Grammar: review of the A1 backbone; repair unstable points before expansion.
- Vocabulary: consolidate the most useful words from Units 1-11 and fill practical gaps.
- Output: complete a short self-introduction, describe a routine, handle a café or shopping exchange, arrange a plan, ask for directions, and give a brief past update.

## Goal adaptation

- **Everyday communication:** prioritize high-frequency functional scenes and written interaction.
- **Travel:** prioritize transport, accommodation, food, services, directions, problems, and plans.
- **Work or study:** prioritize messages, schedules, requests, explanations, domain vocabulary, and register.
- **Exam:** after official requirements are verified, map assessed written abilities to the route and keep unsupported modalities explicitly outside evidence.
- **Custom:** translate the learner's real situations into observable written abilities.

## Scope

This workspace trains reading, grammar in context, writing, and written interaction. It does not measure listening, pronunciation, or spoken production.
